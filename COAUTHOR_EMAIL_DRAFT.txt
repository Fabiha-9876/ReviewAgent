THE SHORT VERSION

Our headline downstream result was wrong. We reported that supplying the IssueSpec to the
response generator raised human-rated reply quality by +2.35 Likert points. The two conditions in
that experiment were not produced by the same model. One came from a template composer that
emitted lemmatised text averaging 78 words, the other from a composer that produced polished
prose averaging 123 words with cleanup passes the first never received. A rater comparing them
was separating two writing styles, not measuring the specification.

I re-ran it with the generator held fixed. The gain is +0.04, p = 0.54. It is a null. The three
raters also disagree in direction, and I am the only one who prefers the specification arm.

WHAT ELSE CHANGED

Three further claims are withdrawn:
  - the cross-family quality difference (+0.38, p = 0.015), which fails multiplicity correction
    over the seven tests it belongs to
  - the compatibility internal F1 of 0.74, because 194 of the 200 synthetic training rows carry
    capital letters and not one of the 215,583 corpus rows does, so the model had a free cue
  - the intrinsic cluster-quality comparison, which compared two designs never run on the same
    reviews

A Krippendorff alpha we reported as 0.451 is actually 0.285. Our implementation was crediting
each rating with a coincidence against itself.

The title is now "Typed Issue Specifications from App Reviews: What Scales, and a Downstream
Benefit That Does Not Replicate". The paper is now an honest negative-replication paper. What
survives is the typed intermediate representation and the verified-anchor procedure.

There are seventeen numbered corrections in NOTICE_CORRECTION.md in the repository. I would ask
each of you to read that file rather than take my summary for it.
